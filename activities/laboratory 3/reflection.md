# Mission 3 Reflection: Become a Multi-Cloud Explorer

This reflection summarizes the learning experience of exploring and comparing major public cloud platforms.

---

## Guided Reflection Questions

### 1. Which cloud platform impressed you the most?

The Amazon Web Services (AWS) impressed me the most due to its maturity and extensive service catalog. Being the pioneer of public cloud computing, it offers a deep selection of tools, documentation, and community resources. This makes it incredibly reliable for deploying almost any type of application, from simple hostings to complex enterprise systems.

### 2. What similarities exist among AWS, Azure, and GCP?

All three platforms share identical core cloud architecture models and service types. They each provide virtual machines for computing, object storage for unstructured data, managed SQL databases, and robust identity access management. Furthermore, they all run on globally distributed infrastructures consisting of regions, availability zones, and edge locations to ensure low latency.

### 3. How do business requirements influence cloud platform selection?

This is purely my personal opinion but I believe business requirements are the primary driver for all architectural choices. A company with a strict budget might choose AWS or GCP for developer-friendly pricing and startup credits, while a university with existing Windows licensing will choose Azure for native integrations. The choice must match the client's current software ecosystem and future scaling needs.

### 4. What did you learn from acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant?

Well from 'being a consultant' - Acting as a consultant taught me to put business objectives ahead of personal technology preferences. I realized that a cloud solutions architect must objectively analyze budget, compliance, and compatibility constraints before proposing solutions. Selecting the most popular cloud provider is meaningless if it does not solve the client's specific problems.

### 5. How has your GitHub Cloud Computing Portfolio improved after completing this mission?

I think my portfolio has/would improve (and further) by adding structured comparison documents, client scenarios, and real-world recommendations. It now demonstrates hands-on research and technical decision-making alongside Linux command usage. This makes the portfolio look highly professional and displays my growth as an aspiring cloud engineer.
